# V9.2 suggestion evaluation log

> **Historical document.** Written for an earlier version, before the QIE mainnet deployment of 23 Nov 2026. Statements about what has or has not been deployed, which wallets to create and test counts describe that time. Current state: [`FINAL_RELEASE_READINESS.md`](FINAL_RELEASE_READINESS.md).

Each point was checked against the v9.1 source before deciding. **Accepted**: implemented as proposed. **Modified**: implemented in a better form. **Rejected**: not implemented, with the reason and the alternative.

| # | Suggestion | Observation correct? | Decision | What was done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Frontend ignores the factory's minimum cover | **Yes.** `loadFactoryRules()` never read `minCoverageBps`; the create screen offered every level; a creator could pick 50%, sign, and have the transaction revert. Its errors were also swallowed (`.catch(() => {})`), so a failed read looked like "no rules". | **Accepted, extended** | New `CircleFactory.rules()` returns every creation rule in one call, with `FACTORY_VERSION = 92`. `loadFactoryRules()` reads it and, for an older factory, says so instead of assuming a lower minimum. The app tells "older factory" apart from "call failed" by checking the factory bytecode for the `rules()` selector, never by a failed call. A read failure shows the error and keeps Create disabled. Cover buttons below the minimum are disabled and labelled "not allowed here", with a line explaining why. Create stays disabled until the rules are loaded and the cover is allowed. The review step shows the factory's actual rules. **Enforcement:** the contract stays authoritative. `createCircle` is the factory's only creation path, and it checks the minimum in both collateral modes (tested). `SavingsCircle` can still be deployed directly with any cover, but such a circle is not in the factory's `isCircle` list, so the app flags it as not genuine and shows no buttons (tested). |
| 2 | Test evidence doesn't match the reported results | **Yes.** `TEST_EVIDENCE.md` describes a v9 environment where dependencies could not be installed. The v9.1 results were reported in chat but never written into that file, which was my gap. | **Accepted** | `scripts/run-evidence.sh` reproduces everything from a clean copy: `npm ci` from both lockfiles, compile, full suite, mutation check, payoff-matrix regeneration (must be byte-identical), app build, Slither, the local demo and the default-story demo. It writes raw logs to `evidence/`, plus SHA-256 hashes of every source file and the environment. Results are in `TEST_EVIDENCE_V9_2.md`. The old `TEST_EVIDENCE.md` is now marked historical. No linter is configured in the project, so none was run; this is stated, not hidden. |
| 3 | Parity only compared final balances | **Yes.** v9.1 compared only final withdrawable balances and the treasury. | **Accepted, extended** | The simulator now records the full state after every catch-up, every round's contributions, every `settle()` and `finalize()`. The parity test compares, at each of those points: every member's status, paid-out flag, collateral, paid in, collateral used, unpaid entitlement, recorded loss, refund claim, refund paid, missed rounds, penalty and withdrawable balance; plus current round, the all-rounds-settled flag, round fund, free reserve and treasury; plus every quoted catch-up cost (total, current-round part, late fee). Coverage: 14 named scenarios in both collateral modes, 6 precision cases × 2 modes, and 160 random scenarios. The random scenarios use sizes 2–12, every cover, fees 0/0.01%/0.5%/1%, raw base units and whole tokens, and catch-ups during rounds, after an expired deadline and in the cure window. **Mutation check:** `scripts/mutation-sim.mjs` applies 11 deliberate accounting errors one at a time, and all 11 are caught, most at an intermediate step. **Not covered by the simulator:** cancellation before the start, stray-token sweeps, `leave()` and withdrawals. Contract tests cover these, and their state is checked against the token balance after every call. A catch-up with too little approved is not a simulator state: the contract reverts and changes nothing (tested). |
| 3b | Token precision: 6/18 decimals, rounding, small and large values | **Partly.** The contract never reads `decimals()`; all its arithmetic is in base units. "18-decimal" is therefore the same arithmetic at different magnitudes. | **Modified** | Precision cases run base units 1, 3 and 7 (rounding up from fractions of a unit), 1,234,567,891,234, and the maximum `1e15` and `1e15 − 1`, at 12 members and 100% cover in both modes. **Finding:** `MAX_CONTRIBUTION = 1e15` base units is 1 billion QUSDC, but only 0.001 of an 18-decimal token. The contract is only practical for low-decimal stablecoins. The factory is pinned to QUSDC (6 decimals), so this is documented, not changed. The app formats with the token's own decimals and uses bigint throughout. |
| 4 | 100% cover may be an adoption barrier | **Yes, and the root cause is the timing of the collateral, not the cover level.** With upfront collateral, seat 1 of 12 at 100% must lock 11 contributions before round 1. But a member only starts owing the group once it has been paid; before that its risk is leaving early, which the penalty already handles. | **Modified: collateral held back from the pot** | New per-circle option `collateralFromPayout` (on by default in the app). Every seat locks a one-share bond (one share × cover) to join. When a member is paid, the contract holds back enough of that pot to top their collateral up to ceil((N − r) × c × cover), the same backing an upfront member has, and returns it at the end. Results (payoff matrix, both modes simulated and cross-checked): seat 1 of 12 at 100% needs **1 + its first payment instead of 11 + its first payment**; walk-away gain and catch-up cost are **identical** in both modes; at 100% cover a walk-away still gains 0 and nobody loses. **Honest trade-offs, shown in the app:** (a) at 100% cover seat 1 has only about 1.94 of its 12-share pot in hand at its turn, because full cover means early seats get little credit; (b) below 100%, a member who leaves in round 1 forfeits only its bond, not up to one share. **Tiers (Protected / Balanced / Flexible) rejected as on-chain categories.** Every circle is already invite-only by address, so "public" vs "private" can't be enforced or verified on chain; a label would be a frontend claim. Instead: (1) a factory deployment sets an immutable minimum cover; (2) the app calls only 100% "full cover" and never "protected" below it; (3) below 100% the creator must tick a box stating the exact walk-away gain and others' loss for seat 1; (4) every member sees the same figures for their own seat before accepting, and accepting is their on-chain consent. **Answers to the questions:** the minimum economically defensible cover with no walk-away incentive is 100% (gain ≈ (N − 1 − s) × c × (1 − cover)). At 50% the gain is positive: 12 members, seat 1 +5.5c. Reputation can't justify lower collateral, because one person can use many wallets. A reserve or insurance layer would need outside money, and the contract won't invent it. Capital per seat and per policy is in `docs/PAYOFF_MATRIX.md`; uncovered exposure per seat is on every seat card. |
| 5 | Capital requirement preview | **Yes.** v9.1 showed collateral but not "how much do I need to start". | **Accepted** | Every full seat card opens with three answers: **Money to start** (collateral to join + round 1 payment), **Locked until the circle ends** (split into "now" and "held from your pot"), and **Most you could lose if you keep paying** (the worst of the simulated scenarios, labelled as such). The commitment list adds "held back from it as collateral" and "in hand in round N". The create screen has a comparison table for seat 1 across every cover level the factory allows, with columns money to start, locked until the end, in hand at its turn, not backed after its turn, and simulated walk-away gain. All figures come from the contract formula or the parity-tested simulator; none are illustrative. |
| 6 | Factory configuration transparency | **Partly.** The settings were already immutable public variables with no admin. What was missing was a single read and a version marker. | **Accepted** | `rules()` and `FACTORY_VERSION`. No admin powers added: every setting is fixed at deployment and applies only to circles that factory creates; existing circles are never affected. **A new factory deployment is required** (new `createCircle` signature). Note: the factory bytecode is 24,481 bytes, 95 under the 24,576-byte limit, because it embeds the circle's code. Any further circle features would need a separate circle deployer. |
| 7 | One consistent financial story | **Partly.** The shared layer existed, but the accept flow approved `collateralFor(i)`, which would be wrong for a from-pot circle. | **Accepted** | One layer: `sim.js` (state machine) → `economics.js` (`seatCommitment`, `seatRisk`, `capitalComparison`) → `SeatRisk.jsx` / `WhatIf.jsx`, used on the create screen, join note, seat tab and risk tab. The accept button now approves exactly `upfrontCollateral(i)` read from the circle. Contract-derived figures say "read from the circle contract"; simulated lines are labelled as simulations with their assumptions. The circle history explains the new `CollateralHeldFromPot` event in plain words. |

## Deployment and compatibility

- **New factory deployment required.** `createCircle` has an 8th argument (`collateralFromPayout`), and there is a new `rules()` view.
- **SavingsCircle changed** (new optional mode, new `upfrontCollateral()` view, new event). With the mode off, behaviour is identical to v9.1: all earlier tests pass unchanged and the parity test covers both modes. Circles from older factories keep working in the v9.2 app, which reads `collateralFromPayout()` with a fallback of "upfront".
- Nothing was deployed to QIE mainnet and no funds were moved.

## Decision for you

`scripts/deploy-factory.js` still defaults a production factory to a 100% minimum cover. With collateral from the pot, 100% now costs one share to join, so the capital barrier is gone. What remains is the credit trade-off in row 4. If you want trust circles below 100% on mainnet, deploy with `MIN_COVER_BPS=0`; the app then requires the risk acknowledgement for those circles.
