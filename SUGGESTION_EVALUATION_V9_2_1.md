# V9.2.1 suggestion evaluation log

> **Historical document.** Written for an earlier version, before the QIE mainnet deployment of 23 Nov 2026. Statements about what has or has not been deployed, which wallets to create and test counts describe that time. Current state: [`FINAL_RELEASE_READINESS.md`](FINAL_RELEASE_READINESS.md).

**Accepted**: the observation is correct and the proposed solution was implemented. **Modified**: the observation is correct, and a better or more complete solution was implemented. **Rejected**: not implemented, with the reason.

## Your direction

I agree with it, with two additions that came out of doing it. First, the deeper lifecycle check (Part 4) found a second, larger difference between the collateral modes than the one you reported: a member who left in round 1 forfeited less in from-pot mode. I fixed it with a one-line change to the join amount. Second, the mainnet RPC is reachable from here, so besides preparing for testnet, I ran the exact production configuration against the **real QUSDC contract** on a local copy of QIE mainnet. That is the closest thing to a real deployment possible without deploying.

## Decisions

| Part | Your observation | Correct? | Decision | What was done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Collateral restored on a late repayment targets the current round, not the member's payout position | **Yes.** `_payOut()` used `ceil((N − r) × c × cover)` with the round of the repayment. Also found: a catch-up at the start of a round targeted one round too few. | **Accepted, extended** | The target is now always `collateralFor(seat)`, the same amount an upfront member of that seat holds, for the original payout and every later repayment. Holding continues while the member still owes a scheduled payment (some round unsettled and the last round not yet paid by them), and collateral never exceeds the target. **Reach of the old bug, measured:** it needs a payout so short that the hold itself is cut short. That never happened in 20,000 random scenarios. In an exhaustive search of 842,970 small-circle scenarios it happened in 106, all built around members leaving in round 1 with a fee; the worst was seat 2 left at 298 instead of 300 (100% cover, 1% fee). Final results were identical in all of them; the gap was in backing during the circle, which matters only if that member later defaults. |
| 1 | Regression tests A–E | Yes | **Accepted** | `test/SavingsCircle.v921.test.js` with exact numbers. **A**: 5 members, 100%, 1% fee, seats 3–5 leave in round 1, so seat 2's pot is 300 short and its collateral stops at 298 of 300. **B**: the round-3 repayment of 200 restores it to exactly 300 (the old rule left 298), and the last 100 repaid is not held. **C**: 6 members; the shortfall is repaid in two parts (2 in a catch-up, 298 in the next settle), and collateral goes 397 → 398.98 → exactly 400, never above. **D**: the same scenarios in upfront mode: collateral equals the seat amount throughout, and no hold event exists. **E**: the last seat never has collateral held; at 0% cover nothing is ever held, even with short payouts and repayments. Plus: nothing is held once a member has no payment left. |
| 1 | Accounting invariants after every repayment | Yes | **Accepted, generalised** | `test/circleRunner.js` checks three rules on the contract's own state after **every transaction** of every parity, regression and lifecycle test. R1: collateral ≤ the seat amount. R2: a member in good standing holds exactly min(seat amount, locked to join + pot money credited after fee while still owing). R3: each credit splits exactly into held + fee + withdrawable; each repayment lowers the entitlement by exactly its amount; in a catch-up the reserve moves by exactly (uncovered + late fee − repayments). Token balance = ledgers is checked after every call, as before. |
| 2 | Parity can pass while contract and simulator share a mistake | **Yes.** All 107 v9.2 tests still passed after the Part 1 fix, which proves they couldn't tell the two rules apart. | **Accepted** | The rule was stated first (above). The simulator's payout logic was rewritten from that statement, not copied from Solidity. The R1–R3 checks are independent of the simulator. Mutation check, now 14 mutations: the 12 simulator-only errors are caught by parity, including the old v9.2 rule in the simulator alone. The 2 new **shared** mutations put the same error into the contract **and** the simulator in a throwaway copy of the project ("target set by the round" and "repayments never top up"): parity agrees, and R2 fails both. 14/14 caught. |
| 3 | Factory bytecode has 95 bytes of headroom | Yes | **Accepted (separate deployer)** | New `CircleDeployer`, created by the factory in its constructor. It records the factory as its only caller and has no owner, settings or other function; `deploy` from anyone else reverts (tested), so minimum cover can't be bypassed through it. Its address is immutable in the factory, so it can't be swapped. Circles still name the factory as registry, the factory still registers them, and events are unchanged. No new admin or trust. Directly deploying a `SavingsCircle` was always possible; such circles are not in `isCircle` and the app doesn't trust them (tested). **Sizes** (solc 0.8.39, optimizer on, 200 runs, EVM paris, no viaIR), runtime against the 24,576 limit: SavingsCircle 15,905 (8,671 spare), CircleFactory 6,492 (18,084 spare), CircleDeployer 18,990 (5,586 spare). Init code against EIP-3860's 49,152: CircleFactory 26,247 (it contains the deployer's). A test fails if any runtime comes within 4 KB of the limit. Simpler alternatives considered: fewer optimizer runs gives a few hundred bytes, not kilobytes; a clone/proxy pattern would add upgrade-like complexity to immutable circles. |
| 3 | Deployment test with the exact production configuration | Yes | **Accepted** | `scripts/fork-production-check.js` runs on a local copy of QIE mainnet with the real QUSDC and `deploy-factory.js`'s production settings: factory deployment (5,575,199 gas), then a full from-pot circle at 100% cover with a default, collateral paying, a catch-up, finalize and withdrawals. All exact; the circle ends empty. |
| 4 | Check the from-pot mode over the whole lifecycle | **Yes, and it found more than expected** | **Accepted, with a fix** | All 15 situations were run in both modes, plus an exhaustive comparison of 1,011,564 small-circle scenarios. **Found:** with v9.2's join bond (one share × cover), a member who left before paying anything forfeited only that bond. That cost members who kept paying up to 1.6 contributions (3–5 members, below 100% cover). **Fixed:** the join amount is now min(seat collateral, one share), so leaving early costs exactly what it costs in upfront mode. That's at most one share to join, never more. **Remaining difference, documented:** only when a member stops before its payout **and** another stops after its payout without catching up. Then upfront mode's larger leftover collateral helps repay members who are owed. A member who kept paying was at most 1 contribution worse off (50% cover), and never worse off at 100% or 0–20%. The short-payout backing gap exists until repaid, but changed no final result. See `docs/MODE_COMPARISON.md` (generated, reproducible) and `test/ModeLifecycle.test.js` (every situation on the contract in both modes, with rule checks and balances matched to the simulator). **So the modes are not identical, and the docs no longer say they are.** |
| 5 | Run the exhaustive suite | Yes | **Accepted, extended** | Run with `EXHAUSTIVE=1` for **both** collateral modes (7,776 patterns × 3 cover levels × 2 modes = 46,656 full circles). Raw logs, source hashes and timings are in `evidence/`. Results: see `TEST_EVIDENCE_V9_2_1.md`. |
| 6 | Users must not confuse gross pot, held collateral and withdrawable money | **Partly.** The v9.2 seat card showed "receives (after fee)", but the gross pot wasn't shown, and history said "received the pot" for the gross amount. | **Accepted** | Seat card: pot, service fee, held back as collateral, and "you can withdraw in round N" as separate lines, plus collateral returned at the end. History: pot and repayment lines say "before the fee and any collateral held back". Members table: collateral shown against the seat's full amount ("1 of 2, the rest held from their pot"). Create screen explains the remaining mode difference in one sentence. No redesign. |
| 7 | Prepare QIE testnet validation | Yes | **Accepted, with honest gaps** | `scripts/testnet-deploy.js` refuses any chain but 1983, deploys the factory (and the project's mock token if no `TOKEN` is given), reads the rules back, checks the deployer link and saves the addresses. It was dry-run on a local chain reporting 1983. `DEPLOYMENT_READINESS_V9_2_1.md` has the step-by-step and checklist. **Could not verify from here:** the testnet RPC (it reset the connection from this machine), the chain ID from QIE's own docs (only a third-party listing confirms 1983), a testnet stablecoin, explorer and faucet. None were invented. |

## Compatibility and deployment

- **New factory deployment required** (corrected rule, new join amount, deployer). Nothing has been deployed; there are no live v9.2 circles to migrate.
- **v9.2 circles, if any existed:** they keep working in this app (same events and views; the app reads `upfrontCollateral()` from each circle, so their own join amounts are used). Their contract still has the v9.2 rule and bond; that can't be changed after deployment.
- Upfront-mode behaviour is unchanged from v9.2 (all earlier upfront tests pass unchanged).
